Bathroom Water Damage in The Villages

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In The Villages, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Tiles near the tub that have loosened, shift when stepped on, or sound hollow
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.
