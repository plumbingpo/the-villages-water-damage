Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in The Villages

Think of drywall as a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

What It Looks Like

  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Water Next Door

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Wet Sheathing Outside

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
