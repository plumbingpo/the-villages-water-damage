Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in The Villages

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our The Villages crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether the platform or subfloor under the tank has to be rebuilt before a new heater goes in.
  • How many stored belongings got wet and need to be moved, dried or hauled away.
  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.
  • Time that passed with the tank still refilling, up to the point the supply was shut.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
