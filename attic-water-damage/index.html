Attic Water Damage Help in The Villages

Water reaches an attic from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. Our team in The Villages inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
  • Water dripping from, or staining around, a ceiling light or bathroom fan
  • Musty odor in upstairs closets or in the attic itself
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold Under the Roof Deck

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

Water Near Electrical

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
