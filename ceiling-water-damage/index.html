Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in The Villages

The stain overhead comes at the end of the story, not the beginning. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
  • Popcorn or knockdown texture that flakes, drops or turns darker
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Water Travels Sideways

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

Wet Lights and Wiring

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. Water in a fixture can short the circuit, so switch off the breaker for that room.

Mold Above You

The top side of the drywall and the insulation resting on it stay wet long after the surface feels dry, which is where mold tends to grow unseen.

Damage on the Floor Above

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

How We Fix It

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.
