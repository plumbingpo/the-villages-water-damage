Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in The Villages

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • A missing or failed float switch that should have shut the system off.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

The Platform and Closet Walls

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Ceilings Under the Unit

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Insulation Around the Unit

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

How We Fix It

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Extract and Open Up

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Clean and Restore

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.
